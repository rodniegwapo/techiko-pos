# Cash & wallet — how money moves, by role

Narration for the explainer video. One `## scene-N` per voiceover clip: `tts.mjs` reads the
**Say:** paragraph of each scene; **Show:** is what the screen does meanwhile. Keep lines short
and plain: they are read aloud. Words match the app's own labels.

## scene-1 — Where did the money go?
**Show:** a peso coin drops into a drawer, then splits into three places: the drawer, a phone (GCash), and the owner's hand. Title: Cash and wallet. Then the five tabs slide in.

**Say:** At the end of a busy day, every store owner asks the same question. Where is my money right now? Cash and wallet answers that. It follows every peso, from the moment a customer pays, to the drawer, to the bank, and to the owner. It lives under five tabs. Today's drawer. Where money is. Cash in and out. Shifts and counts. And Cash on hand. Let's walk through them, one role at a time.

## scene-2 — Three places money lives
**Show:** diagram that grows: Cash drawer (each cashier's tray, inside the store drawer) → Payment channels (card terminal, e-wallet, bank) → Cash on hand (owner, outside every store). Then Expected vs Counted with Short and Over.

**Say:** First, three words you will hear a lot. The cash drawer is the physical cash in the store. Each cashier has their own tray, and together they make the store's drawer. Payment channels are where non cash payments land, like a card terminal, GCash, or a bank. And Cash on hand is the business's cash that the owner holds, outside of every store. One more idea. Expected is what the app says should be in the drawer. Counted is what you actually count. If counted is less, you are short. If it is more, you are over.

## scene-3 — Four roles
**Show:** four role cards build one by one: Cashier (My shift), Supervisor (the whole store drawer, counts), Manager (ends the day, cash in and out), Owner or Admin (every store, Cash on hand, transfers).

**Say:** Four roles use this, and each one sees a different part. A cashier handles only their own shift. A supervisor watches the whole store drawer, and checks the cashiers' counts. A manager does that too, and also ends the store's day, and records money in and out. The owner, or an admin, sees every store, holds Cash on hand, and is the only one who moves money between stores.

## scene-4 — Cashier: start, sell, end and count
**Show:** footage. Today's drawer, My shift, Start my shift with five hundred pesos opening cash, a cash sale in Sales, back to My shift figures, End my shift, count the tray, status Needs review.

**Say:** Here is a cashier's day. Open Today's drawer, and under My shift, choose Start my shift. Enter your opening cash, here five hundred pesos. You can also start from the Sales page. Now sell as usual. Every cash sale adds to your tray's Expected. When you are done, choose End my shift, and count the cash in your tray. You don't need a PIN. If your count matches, you are done. If it is short or over, the shift shows Needs review, and a supervisor checks it. You can keep working meanwhile.

## scene-5 — How a shift moves along
**Show:** state diagram that grows: Open → Ended → Needs review → Approved. A loop arrow from Needs review back to Open, labelled Send back for a recount. Side notes: Forgotten shifts, Ended elsewhere.

**Say:** Every shift follows the same path. It starts Open. When the cashier counts, it becomes Ended. If the count is off, it goes to Needs review. A supervisor then approves it, or sends it back for a recount. When it is sent back, the cashier counts again, and the shift goes through the same steps. If a cashier forgets to end their shift, it shows under Forgotten shifts, so someone can end it for them. And if a shift was ended on another device, the Sales page tells you, instead of letting you sell on a closed shift.

## scene-6 — Supervisor: the store's drawer and the counts
**Show:** footage. Today's drawer summary cards: Cash in drawers, Cash sales today, Card, e-wallet and bank, Counts off. Spot check my drawer. Then Shifts and counts, Needs review, Approve with a note.

**Say:** A supervisor opens Today's drawer and sees the whole store. Cash in drawers is every tray added up. Cash sales today is what went into the drawers. Card, e-wallet and bank is money that went past the drawer, straight into a channel. Counts off shows how many counts need a review. A supervisor can set the opening cash, and do a spot check any time. A spot check counts the drawer without ending anything. Then, on Shifts and counts, open a count that needs review. Look at Expected, Counted, and the Difference. Approve it with a note, or send it back for a recount. A supervisor only sees their own team.

## scene-7 — Manager: end the store's day
**Show:** footage. Today's drawer, End the store's day, Final count, then the two choices: Cash out and Keep as opening. Then Day closed, and Reopen the day.

**Say:** When every cashier has ended their shift, the manager ends the store's day. Choose End the store's day, and enter the final count. Then decide where the cash goes. Cash out sends it to Cash on hand, for the owner. The drawer starts tomorrow empty. Keep as opening leaves it in the drawer, as tomorrow's opening cash. Once the day is closed, nothing more can be added to it. If you made a mistake, a manager or admin can reopen the day, with a reason.

## scene-8 — Cash in and out at a store
**Show:** footage. Cash in and out, Cash in, Added float, one thousand pesos. Then the list of lines: Sale, Expense paid, Paid supplier, Owner took cash, Moved into channel. Each modal shows the before and after.

**Say:** Not all money comes from sales. On Cash in and out, a manager records the rest. Cash in, for example Added float, when you put in change or small bills. Cash out, for example Owner took cash. Move, to send money between the drawer and the store's own channels, like depositing cash into the bank. Before you save, the app shows the balance before and after, so you see exactly what changes. Other pages add lines here too. An expense, a supplier payment, or a loan payment all show up, because they also took money from the drawer.

## scene-9 — Where money is
**Show:** footage. Where money is: Money in each store now, grouped as Cash, E-wallets, Banks and card terminals, with subtotals; then All places.

**Say:** Where money is puts it all on one screen. For each store, you see the cash drawer, every e-wallet, and every bank and card terminal, each with its balance and a subtotal. The owner sees every store side by side, plus Cash on hand. This is the answer to the question we started with. Where is my money right now?

## scene-10 — Owner: allow money between stores
**Show:** footage. Inventory, Locations, edit Makati, turn on Can transfer money, save. Same for SM Mall.

**Say:** Now the owner's part. Money only moves between stores that allow it. Go to Inventory, then Locations. Edit each store, and turn on Can transfer money. Do this for both stores. One more rule. You can't move money into or out of a day that has already been ended.

## scene-11 — Three ways to move money to another store
**Show:** diagram that grows, one path at a time. Way 1: Store A → Store B, straight arrow, labelled Transfer. Way 2: Store A drawer → owner's hand → Store B drawer, labelled Owner took cash, into another store's drawer. Way 3: Store A → Cash on hand → Store B, labelled Move to a store. Then quick footage of each.

**Say:** There are three ways to move money to another store. Only one of them passes through Cash on hand. One. Transfer, on Cash in and out. Pick the other store and the place, drawer or channel, and the money goes straight there. Two. Owner took cash, and choose Into the other store's drawer. The owner carries the cash over. This only works from the cash drawer. Three. Through Cash on hand. The cash comes in first, and later the owner chooses Move to a store, and picks its drawer or one of its channels.

## scene-12 — Cash on hand, in depth
**Show:** footage. Cash on hand: the balance, Waiting to collect, by store, Collect an approved shift, Collected today, Moved to stores today, then Move to a store and Owner used it.

**Say:** Cash on hand is the owner's safe. Money gets in two ways. When a manager chooses Cash out at the end of the day. Or when the owner collects an approved shift, from Waiting to collect. Money goes out three ways. Move to a store sends it back to a drawer or channel. Correction fixes a mistake. And Owner used it means the owner spent it outside the business. Remember this. Collecting cash, and moving it back to a store, does not change the books. It is still the business's money. Only Owner used it takes money out of the business.

## scene-13 — Cash flow: money in and out of the business
**Show:** footage. Finance, Money in and out: Where did my money go? The summary cards, the statement with Money in and Money out, Bank and e-wallet balances, and Why profit and money are different.

**Say:** Finally, the big picture. Under Finance, Money in and out shows your cash flow. Money in is sales, credit payments, loans, and money the owner put in. Money out is expenses, suppliers, loan payments, equipment, and owner draws. Moving money between your own drawers, channels, and Cash on hand is not on this list, because it never left the business. And there is a section called Why profit and money are different. You can make a profit, and still be short on cash, if it is tied up in stock or in customer credit.

## scene-14 — Which one, and recap
**Show:** a table: Need it there now → Transfer. Carrying cash to another drawer → Owner took cash. Collect first, decide later → Cash on hand. Then the four role cards again, then the end card.

**Say:** So, which way to move money? If the other store needs it now, use Transfer. If you are carrying cash from one drawer to another, use Owner took cash. If you collect first and decide later, use Cash on hand. To recap. Cashiers start, sell, and count their own tray. Supervisors check the drawer and the counts. Managers end the store's day and record money in and out. And the owner sees every store, keeps Cash on hand, and moves money between stores. That's Cash and wallet.
